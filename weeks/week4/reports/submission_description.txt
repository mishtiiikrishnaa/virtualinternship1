This report integrates the three previous weeks of the internship into one
decision-support document. On the seeded development extract of four thousand
two hundred shipments shipped in twenty twenty five, the ridge demand forecast
reached a test mean absolute error of two point eight shipments per day against
three point five four for a naive forecast, an improvement of twenty point nine
percent. The forecast interval half width is about six point eight shipments
per day, and the empirical coverage of that band on the held-out window was
ninety two point eight six percent.

The late-delivery classifier flags shipments that are likely to miss their
promise. At the chosen threshold of zero point zero five it caught forty six
point seven percent of late shipments at a precision of eleven point seven
percent, with a ROC area under the curve of zero point nine. The expected cost
of a missed late shipment is fifteen hundred dollars and the cost of a wasted
escalation is sixty dollars.

The report recommends planning capacity against the forecast plus its interval,
reviewing the triage threshold as the cost ratio moves, reporting service
performance mode by mode, and retraining quarterly. Every recommendation is tied
to a number in the results file and audited against it.

The development extract is seeded rather than live, so these are proof of
concept findings about that extract. The decision framework is written for use
on top of a retrained model and a re-validated label.
