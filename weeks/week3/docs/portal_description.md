i built the week 3 pipeline on the week 1 cleaned extract: 4,200 shipments shipped during 2025. the task was two linked models. first, forecast daily shipment demand 14 to 28 days ahead. second, classify individual shipments as late or on time, which is what makes a confusion matrix worth drawing. for the forecast i used strictly trailing features: daily volume lags, seven, fourteen and twenty eight day rolling means, calendar features and trailing mode shares. the model never sees its own future value. for the classifier i used booking time information only: mode, carrier, lane distance and city, weight, quantity, freight cost, ship day and month, plus missing value flags. delivery date, transit days and delay days are consequences of the outcome and are banned; tests enforce it. after time ordered splits and timeseries split tuning, ridge cut the naive baseline from 3.54 to 2.80 shipments per day on the twenty eight day test window, s m a p e 23.9 percent. the class weighted booster reached r o c auc 0.91 and p r auc 0.11, recalling 47 percent at precision 0.11 on threshold tuned for validation f1. rule of thumb: plan two weeks ahead and treat the classifier as triage, not a guarantee. strong predictors are lane, mode and day of week, not weight or cost. limits: seeded sample not telemetry, one annual cycle, the late class is 1.7 percent and there is no weather. these appear in report title, figure titles and the limitation table. next week extends this to scheduled retraining and a weather feed rather than a new narrative.
